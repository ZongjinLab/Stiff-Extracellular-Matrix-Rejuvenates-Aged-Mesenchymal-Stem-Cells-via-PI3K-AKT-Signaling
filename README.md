# Stiff-Extracellular-Matrix-Rejuvenates-Aged-Mesenchymal-Stem-Cells-via-PI3K-AKT-Signaling
Abstract
Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are widely used to modulate immune responses and promote tissue regeneration, but cellular senescence significantly impairs their therapeutic potential. Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) undergo senescence characterized by an enlarged morphology, reduced proliferative capacity, and altered expression of genes related to the cytoskeleton. This study revealed that the mechanical microenvironment critically regulates MSC aging. Culturing senescent MSCs on high-stiffness extracellular matrix (ECM) hydrogels reverses key aspects of the senescent phenotype, including the restoration of normal morphology, improved cytoskeletal integrity, and enhanced proliferation. This mechanical rescue is accompanied by genetic reprogramming and activation of the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway. The inhibition of PI3K-Akt with LY294002 abolished these rejuvenating effects. These findings demonstrate that the mechanical microenvironment can reprogram senescent MSCs through PI3K-AKT-mediated mechanotransduction, offering a novel strategy to restore stem cell function in aging and regenerative medicine.

Experimental design
To investigate how matrix-provided mechanical cues regulate the transcriptomic state of senescent mesenchymal stem cells, human umbilical cord-derived mesenchymal stem cells (UC-MSCs) were continuously expanded on tissue culture polystyrene until passages 14–16 to establish replicative senescence. Senescent UC-MSCs were then cultured under three mechanical conditions: tissue culture polystyrene (TCPS control), soft decellularized extracellular matrix (dECM; approximately 7–9 kPa), and relatively stiff dECM (approximately 39–41 kPa). The dECM substrates provided distinct mechanical resistance environments to cell-generated traction forces. Cells were seeded at the same density and cultured under identical conditions before RNA extraction. Total RNA was collected from independent biological replicates in each group and subjected to RNA sequencing. Transcriptomic profiles were compared among the TCPS, soft dECM, and stiff dECM groups to identify mechanical cue-responsive genes and signaling pathways associated with cytoskeletal organization, proliferation, cellular senescence, and mechanotransduction.
